| **A confined program cannot make a session of its own.** `setsid` is answered as done and not done, so that every process a command starts stays in its session, which the tree's end kills ([decision 0032](../decisions/0032-what-the-linux-sandbox-needs.md)). It costs every program that makes a terminal (the child of `forkpty` exits: Python's `pty.spawn`, pexpect, node-pty; `script` runs without one), and every program that signals the session it believes it made as a group (Node's `process.kill(-pid)` after `detached: true` finds none; Python's `os.killpg(os.getpgid(pid))` after `start_new_session=True` kills the whole command). The way out today is a run outside the sandbox with approval. Found by the second review of step 26, which also found that faking `setpgid` broke `timeout`; that one is allowed again. **The child subreaper belongs with step 32, not here.** The kill now sweeps the whole session (`tree::unix::kill_session`), so a process in a group of its own is reached, and a process that outlives the kill is caught by the look before the next command ([the-last-writes-of-a-killed-command.md](the-last-writes-of-a-killed-command.md)). Letting `setsid` work needs more than this item: a process with a session of its own leaves the sweep's reach, so it must be paired with a subreaper and a control-group kill, which is the hardening step's work. **Recommendation (for step 32):** let `setsid` work; make the harness the child subreaper (`PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER`) so a re-parented process is still known; and freeze and kill the tree with a control group rather than by session.
